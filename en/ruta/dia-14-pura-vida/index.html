Chapter 14 · Day 14 · 7 Nov 2026

Pura vida

San Juan del Sur → Liberia · Costa Rica · 122 km traveled

We finally made it to Costa Rica. Nicaragua saw us off along the Peñas Blancas route with some spectacular wind turbines. It's the second wind farm I've seen on the trip, and you can't imagine how gigantic they are until you're right next to one and can see how huge they are.

1/2

It was a cloudy, stormy, rainy day, but nice because it wasn't so hot. At breakfast, the man I assume is the hotel owner apologized to us because the weather wasn't ideal, and we were thrilled because it was cloudy. I told him: "Don't worry, that's how we like the beach, without much sun, so we don't get burned". He said: "It's just that other tourists like sun and beach", and I was like: hahaha, not us, it's lovely honestly.

We filled the car's tank for roughly 450 quetzales, a bit less than I'd pay in Guatemala. So yes, gas is cheaper in Nicaragua too.

When we got to the border, being newbies, we had to wait almost three times as long. So here are some tips for taking a car out of Nicaragua into Costa Rica through the Peñas Blancas border (I'll do a tutorial for entering later):

1. Bring photocopies of your passport.

2. Bring cash, about 35,000 colones, which you'll need in Costa Rica. You can exchange with people at the border, but they'll charge you an arm and a leg on the rate, so better bring about 50 or 60 dollars.

3. At the Nicaraguan border, the municipality of Rivas charges 1 dollar per person if you're not Nicaraguan.

4. If you're driving, there are several steps. I recommend this order:

4.1. Before entering the border compound there's a police officer who gives you a declaration form. Fill it out as you normally would when leaving or entering any country (funny, because at Peñas Blancas the form was different from the one at El Guasaule).

4.2. Do your regular immigration process with your passport and all. If you're a foreigner, even from Central America, you pay 3 dollars at the window; again, bring cash, because nothing can be paid by card.

4.3. After getting your exit stamp and filling out the declaration, go out the door and around the compound, right where you first parked the car. There's an inspection booth you have to visit: they'll ask for the declaration, passport, driver's license and car papers, and then inspect the car.

4.4. Then they sign off the inspection and you go to the police next door, who will ask for the same papers and sign again.

4.5. Now comes the tricky part: go in through immigration and ask where the vehicle paperwork window is. I had to use the one on the Nicaragua entry side. Since we were carrying a bicycle, we also went to customs (goods) and they signed again; that's the third signature. Here you have to leave a photocopy of your passport.

4.6. Go to the last check, the window next to it, and if you have everything, you can finally leave Nicaragua.

I did all this running around in the rain and it took about 2 hours, so don't be like me.

We moved to the Costa Rican side, and it's one of those things every Central American already knows: Ticos have their pride HAHAHAHA, and rightly so. We arrived at a covered terminal with shiny ceramic floors, a friendly guard, everyone friendly, and immigration took us 10 minutes. Here, we were the ones who made the mistakes.

What you need to enter Costa Rica with a Guatemalan car:

1. Enter the border and do the regular immigration process.

2. Go to the window, where they'll give you a form to fill out.

3. Pay the insurance, about 31,000 colones, roughly 60 dollars.

4. Go back to the window so they can inspect the car.

5. The Vehitur window certifies you and gives you some papers. Don't lose them.

6. Welcome to Costa Rica!

We spent 4 hours on paperwork: on the Nicaraguan side because we didn't know the flow, and on the Costa Rican side because we didn't have enough cash. At the border they exchanged my quetzales for 30,000 colones; I stood in line and everything, and it turns out it's now 31,000, so I had to go to the ATM. The officers, very kindly, told me where and how to find it. Since I'd already officially entered Costa Rica, I could leave and come back through immigration: there's a mini mall with a Banco de Costa Rica ATM. I withdrew 5,000 colones because I was short a thousand HAHAHAHA, about 2 dollars. Noooooooooooooo, hahaha. So today's walking goal was met: my feet hurt.

We got to the first border at 12 and headed out toward Liberia around 4 in the afternoon. We stopped at a wonderful restaurant called Brasas, in La Cruz, which I highly recommend, mainly because they take cards. Here's the map:

Brasas on the map →

We had lunch and got back on the road. People drive much faster here, it's incredible: we went from 50 km/h to over 100 km/h. We stayed calm as always, but we could see cars easily going twice as fast.

We finally reached our lodging, a charming place José found on Airbnb. Tomorrow I'll tell you more about the thoughts I've got stuck in my head, but we're heading to Puntarenas and then waiting for the event from the 20th to the 22nd.

It feels really nice to finally be close to reaching the goal. My knee hurts, my feet hurt, but here we are. It feels so good to be almost done with what we set out to do; now all that's left is the way back, but that'll be a project for future Yeffri.

Thanks for reading. And I want to ask you: would you do all this for the adventure, because you're crazy, or would you rather stay home?
