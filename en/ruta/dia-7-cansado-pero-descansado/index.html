Tired, but rested

Chinandega · Nicaragua

On day 7 we ate and rested. It's really interesting how these rest days keep piling up, just like the fatigue.

I got up early or late, I don't even remember, but before 9, because that's when the hotel stops serving the complimentary breakfast. And then I went back to bed, because magically I was dehydrated: maybe from having the air conditioning on all night, or from not drinking enough water, I got thirsty during the night.

I spent a few hours drifting around on my phone, which I think might be the cause of my lack of focus, so I'll be working on that in the coming days. But the problem, dear readers, is that nobody can be perfect, much less in the stressful situations we run into, not only at work but in everyday life. We do what we need to distract ourselves and not go crazy in the process.

José went off to work and I joined him at the café around noon. We sat there talking and working. I ordered an "extreme lemonade" or something like that, which said "lemonade with a hint of mint", HAHA, and an Oreo cake. It came to about 80 córdobas; I'm still not used to switching between currencies, and we still had no cash. The lemonade was more mint than lemon, haha, not my thing. The Oreo cake was interesting, because I thought it would be a soaked cake or something like that, but it was fine: normal and pretty sweet.

Then we went to have lunch. I'd been looking up places in Chinandega, but José already knew where we were going: Mamá Ofelia. Chinandega is a kind of colonial coastal city, with wide, grid-like streets that are pretty well kept; it seems they had just renovated a few of them. It has a nice, wide road connecting it to town.

We stopped by the hotel, supposedly to drop our things off, but as it turns out, it was closed. That's right: the hotel we were staying at had its doors shut, HAHAHAHA. I imagine they went to lunch or out for food; something happened, but nobody was there, not even the security guard.

So we went with our backpacks to the comedor, which was a kind of buffet. We got there a bit early, or during the shift change, because there wasn't much, but by the time we finished eating everything was nicely set out and ready. We ordered meat, cheese, "maduro con queso", which is plantain with cheese inside, and "tajadas", and they always add pickled vegetables. The food here has a citrusy flavor, like they put lime on everything.

We ate and chatted for a bit, but the heat was intense, so we went back to the hotel to rest. Not for long, because we were going to Restaurante Blanquita with José's friends and family.

There I met a 19-year-old who's studying accounting but got a technical degree in electronics, and the only thing they taught him was how to repair irons and do wiring. I always carry a robot head in the car, Juanito's, so I showed it to him. And he says: "This runs on Arduino, right?" "Yep, there it is." And he says: "Ahh, I'd never seen one." And I'm like… how interesting, how interesting. My mind wandered off, and once again I realized where I was standing.

We went to the pool and had dinner/lunch, haha, I don't know what it was, but we ate. After that, José's friends, who hadn't seen him in about 20 years, were so excited that we went from place to place, and the places kept closing, because everything closes around 8. We went to a shopping center where we finally found an ATM and could withdraw money: I took out 500 córdobas, about 100 quetzales. Since the mall café was closed, we went back to the hotel café, and we'd just arrived when they started tallying up the bills, haha, and we were like: "No way," HAHAHA.

Anyway, we ate and talked, and from my shorts I pulled out an ESP32 Super Mini, one of the most advanced chips of its kind today. José and I told our friend's son: "If you figure out what this is and get it working, by the time we come back you'll have open doors in Guatemala." I think it's important to point out that here it's more about access to things, about seeing how curious the kid is and, above all, that he doesn't give up, because his dad has a lot of faith in him, and challenges like this are what he'll always face as long as he's in tech.

We dropped the family off and stayed chatting for another half hour or forty minutes, until we finally went back to the hotel. While I was in the pool, I noticed the backs of my knees felt kind of tender; I swam quite a bit and that made them hurt, they were sensitive.

I told José that it's incredible that, despite everything his friend told us, you can see his drive to keep going: to keep working, to keep living. That's what I take away. It's a big lesson to know that I have more privileges and opportunities, and that I shouldn't waste them. José's friend has a beautiful family, three kids and his wife, and you can see the passion in everything he does. That's who I want to be when I grow up.

We went back to the hotel and passed out, and that's how the day ended: tired, but rested.
