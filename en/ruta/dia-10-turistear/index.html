Finally, some sightseeing

Managua · Nicaragua

It's funny how people think we're traveling for fun, on vacation, disconnected from everything and not working. But that's not how it is: you're always working, because making connections is work too. We now have all of Central America lined up to hold events next year under our own brand, and all that's left is to make it official with partnerships, photos and so on.

On Tuesday we finally got to go out, thanks to David in the afternoon and Lua in the evening. David is a friend I made back in 2015–2016, when I was starting out in Guatemala's free software communities. Then it turned out there was a Central America–wide community, and the first time we all got together was in 2016, in Costa Rica. I recommend joining the Telegram groups, @software_libre_gt and @slcentroamerica, and telling them you heard about them from yeffrimic.

Telegram group: Software Libre Guatemala →

Telegram group: Software Libre Centroamérica →

We had lunch at Ecorustik and, for the first time, I ate a pizza with a plantain base instead of flour. It was really good. I ordered one with puyazo (a cut of beef), and José ordered the pizza, HAHAHAHAHA. So good, honestly. It's the kind of thing you only find locally in each country, very authentic. If you travel to Nicaragua, you have to try it.

Then we went to the historic center, where the National Palace is. Downtown Managua is quite picturesque, with lots of Trees of Life, flowers and, above all, very clean.

We reached the old cathedral and suddenly memories of Zelda came back, specifically Breath of the Wild, where one of the first places you visit is a ruined church. It looked a lot like it. This whole historic part of the country is beautiful. I took lots of photos, which I'm leaving here.

Then we went to the Tiscapa viewpoint, on a hill in the middle of Managua where you can see everything. The photos don't do justice to how beautiful it is. Next to the viewpoint is the Tiscapa lagoon, and there were giant prints of historical figures.

David, José and I were chatting. It's really interesting how people who don't know each other, but have things in common, can get along so well. I hadn't seen David in a long time: the last time was in 2019, when I came for Mozilla Activate and we were at his house with Lexo, another friend. It was so hot that day that we bought ourselves a beef soup to feel better, HAHAHA. We were LYIIING, LYING on the floor, because it was the coolest spot in the house, which he'd just bought, and there we were being silly as always, HAHAHAHAHA.

We went back to the hotel. I had no idea eating so much plantain would make us so tired. At 7 we had to meet Lua at El Chante, one of the ones in Managua, so we rested for a bit. I almost fell asleep, but I called José and we headed out.

I have to add a caveat here: in my previous post I talked about how good the roads are, and that's true of the main ones, which have clearly been a priority; other streets are simpler. We arrived without much traffic. It's really interesting, because we haven't hit traffic anywhere in Nicaragua, maybe because we travel at what I call odd hours, but nothing like Guatemala City.

We got to talk with Lua, whom I hadn't met in person, but who came to last year's Arduino Day; there's even a photo, HAHAHA! Here I realized once again that food in Nicaragua is plentiful and quite a bit cheaper than in Guatemala: we ate a ton at lunch, and now I couldn't finish a bowl of shredded beef. That said, the cocoa, chocolate with milk, has been some of the best I've ever had.

We talked about communities, projects and the partnerships we're going to build next year. So yes: Lua is now our official ally in Nicaragua, and she's going to help us run more and better events. I didn't finish my food, we recorded the day's video and went to bed early, because the next day we had to leave at 7 a.m.

I think this was the day I allowed myself to take photos and be a tourist. The previous days weren't bad, but today we really rested, explored and played tourist, because we've been working, moving, pedaling, eating and sleeping, and there's no room left for sightseeing. The worst part is that we're both workaholics, so it's tricky.

We're improving the plan little by little. What matters, again, isn't having the perfect plan, but improving it with every iteration, and José and I have been improving it every day. There have been good days, bad days and days more tiring than others, but every day is something new to learn. Let's hope it all goes well. We'll keep building partnerships and growing the movement. Thanks to everyone for reading this far; I hope you enjoy the photos.
