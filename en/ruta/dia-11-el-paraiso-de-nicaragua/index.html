Nicaragua's paradise

Managua → San Juan del Sur · Nicaragua · 145 km traveled

On days when we don't do much, like today, I'll try to work through some reflections. I'm still finding my writing voice, so please forgive me if it isn't as consistent as it should be. I've noticed I also need time and space to put my ideas in the Pensieve, like Dumbledore in Harry Potter: analyze them, dissect them and, of course, sit down and write them. Some days are packed, like Sunday, when we didn't rest, and even today, when we left Managua for San Juan del Sur hoping the police wouldn't stop us again, HAHAHA. The pressure is real, HAHAHA.

On Wednesday we got up early for a meeting with a potential partner, about some projects that have nothing to do with what we're doing on this trip. In the age of the internet and video calls, I now really see the value and the difference of being there in person: video calls can become routine and soulless. So I'm going to start holding in-person meetings most of the time.

We talked about fintechs and investment. And if you need a core banking system, José and I have one: don't forget to message me on Instagram, or José. It was a really fun and enriching meeting; there are people with a lot of experience, and it's always nice to learn about other topics. I've said it before: the flaw of the technologist, the engineer, the genius, is thinking they know everything, that they can solve everything and, above all, that everything should be solved the way they think, without getting their hands dirty: without going there to ask, to look, to apply the scientific method and propose solutions. I hope we can do business in Nicaragua in the near future.

I just spent 30 minutes setting up VS Code so it would recognize my files properly. I write in something called Markdown, a format I love, HAHAHA, but the editor wouldn't recognize it, and it turns out the file had a space at the end of its name. Anyway, moving on.

We went back to the hotel to rest, supposedly to sleep, haha, but it didn't happen: I got to work on my stuff and some projects, and then we headed for San Juan del Sur. I'll be repetitive about the roads, but I'm genuinely surprised by how well marked they are. Obviously not all of them are equally well kept, but the main ones are, and that's something I'd love to see in Guatemala, on the Roosevelt, on the highway to El Salvador or on the Pan-American.

Thank God it rained, which meant fewer people on the road. We always drove at 50 km/h, as the law says. We passed through a beautiful stretch, Nicaraguan plains full of cattle, lots and lots of cattle; we saw small lagoons along the way, everything calm and, best of all, not too hot thanks to the rain. The road was really well kept, quite wide and, above all, with room to ride a bike safely. There were 100-meter stretches of bike lane that were kind of funny, but I told José: "We don't have those 100 meters on any road outside the capital, haha. So they beat us, even if only by 100 meters."

We went through Rivas, José's department, which we thought would be another big city, but it's more of a town: pretty, small, developed, with shopping centers, a city in transition. We kept going toward San Juan del Sur along a gorgeous straight road lined with trees, where you could ride a bike everywhere. I highly recommend it.

We finally arrived in San Juan del Sur and were greeted by a roundabout. I thought Xela had a lot of roundabouts, but the prize goes to Nicaragua, the land of roundabouts. Not that it's bad, hahahaha; I'm just amazed by how many there are, and honestly I really like it.

We stayed at a beautiful hotel, HC Liri, which I recommend 100%. A second-floor terrace welcomed us with a view not only of the pool but of the sea, which, as unbelievable as it seems to me with mountains right next to it, really is salt water. That contrast of mountains and beach always amazes me: Guatemala's beaches are usually far from the mountains, our coast is flatter, so seeing these mountains is really interesting.

HC Liri Hotel on the map →

We had lunch, a fish the size of my kitty, gazing at the sea. How nice to be by the water without too much heat, because there was a storm nearby, and we were happy.

Walking back from lunch to the hotel, we realized we were wearing sweatpants, T-shirts, sneakers and socks, HAHAHAHA. We were at the beach and had no clue: the plan was just to go to lunch and we didn't think about it at all, until I said: "Oh, but you can go into the sea here," hahahah. Then I looked at myself, looked at José and said: "Hmmm, never mind," HAHAHAHA. We didn't go into the sea that day.

The beach is a strange mix of dirt and sand with lots, way too many, stones; they look like river stones, HAHAHA. It's odd to see the stones and then shells, and again, THERE ARE SO MANY, so many you need shoes because it hurts to walk on them. But everything is always very clean.

We didn't have dinner that day because we'd eaten so much. We spent hours in the pool and then went to bed.

It was a good day. It's amazing how clean they keep the beach, how orderly everything looks, and even how the street vendors aren't as pushy as in other places. I'd love for something like this to exist in Guatemala, and I think it's possible if the authorities, national and local, and all of us put some love into our beaches.

I went to sleep with a strange feeling.
