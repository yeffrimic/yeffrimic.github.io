Tuanis vos!

San Juan del Sur · Nicaragua

Today was tourist day. It's amazing how beautiful, clean and well kept San Juan del Sur is. For Guatemalans in their thirties (or already a bit past it), the word "tuanis" will bring back their childhood: sometime between the 90s and the 2000s it was used a ton. I remember hearing it, but I never used it.

These days I think Central American culture has been blending together. A very Guatemalan word is "cabal", and I heard it everywhere: every place we asked something, people said "sí, cabal, eso mero" (yep, exactly, that's it), and I was like: "awww, I feel at home".

San Juan del Sur is a little wonder. As I said before, it's really pretty and well kept; it's what Pana (Panajachel) once wanted to be, what El Paredón and any beach in Guatemala would like to be. They have a lovely lookout: they charge 40 córdobas per person, roughly 1 dollar. Bring cash if you go, and if you're going to be in Nicaragua, take out plenty, you'll need it. Everything is pretty cheap and, as I said in earlier posts, food is abundant.

We went to the lookout of the Cristo de la Misericordia (Christ of Mercy), a pretty big statue that, from what I read, was built in gratitude for some event. Inside there's a small chapel and the views are stunning. Just go in sneakers and with good cardio, because the hill is steep. If you drive up you don't have to walk much, about 100 meters, but on a slope that must be around 80 degrees and that really brings tears to your eyes.

Here's proof that even Navi motorcycles make it all the way up HAHAHA. You can't imagine how popular these bikes are all over Central America; we've seen them everywhere HAHA.

We went down to explore San Juan del Sur. They have a really nice boardwalk and, again, well kept. We had lunch at Tuanis: I ordered a shrimp risotto that was spectacular and José a surf and turf, with beef, shrimp and a lobster tail. This one is a bit pricey, though: roughly 30 to 40 dollars per person, to give you an idea. We had dinner there too and then it was work and bed.
