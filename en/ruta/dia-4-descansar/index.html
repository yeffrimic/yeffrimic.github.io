We all need to rest

Soyapango → San Salvador · El Salvador · 20 km traveled

Day four is for resting. All of us, at some point, have to rest, and that's not up for debate. Every time we make a superhuman effort, whether it's thinking hard, sacrificing ourselves at work or, in this case, exercising after such a long time, we need to rest.

Today I got up late, but something interesting happened the night before that I want to share. For several months I've been staying up late watching Naruto. It's an anime that relaxes me, that I like and, above all, that inspires me: there's something in Japanese philosophy and its way of seeing things that inspires me a lot. I even believe there are studies saying that watching Naruto makes you more resilient. I can confirm it happened to me, but I can't claim it was only because of Naruto in my case.

These last few months I've managed my sleep really badly, and I usually don't fall asleep until I'm truly exhausted. I quit scrolling social media for something I thought was less harmful, but that's like quitting cigarettes and picking up a vape or nicotine gum, HAHA: we just swap one bad habit for another.

I'd gone a couple of days without falling back into that routine, but last night, knowing I could relax today, it felt like a good day to pick those toxic habits back up: falling asleep after midnight and watching anime until I passed out. And I did, as a kind of cheat that gave me dopamine for breaking the rules I set for myself. But today, on day 4, I kept thinking about how easy it is to relapse, how comfortable it is to go back to the things that hurt us while we're happy. And in the end, this trip is about the exact opposite: building new routines, detoxing. Not to become a better person, because we already are, in different ways, but to take better care of myself.

Anyway, I fell asleep late watching Naruto and woke up late because of it. I even woke up at 6 a.m., out of habit, and went to the bathroom because my bladder was about to burst. But instead of staying up, even with nothing to do, I went back to sleep, haha. My brain looks for the most satisfaction with the least effort, and that means sleeping until I can't anymore, like when I was 15.

I remember one September 15th, Guatemala's Independence Day. I played in the school band and didn't want to get up early, because I knew the parade started at 9: why get up early if everyone was going to show up late anyway? It was an endless fight with my mom, in which of course I'd get up angry and offended because she wouldn't let me sleep. Once again my brain saying "why early, if it starts later?" There's a certain pleasure in sleeping in that I still haven't figured out.

I got up at 9 in a rush and José and I went on our morning walk. We had breakfast at Clavo y Canela, a pretty nice restaurant, somewhat pricey, but tasty enough to justify paying 8 dollars for an omelette, which would be about 70 quetzales.

That day José and I split up: he went to lunch and meetings on his own, and I went to reconnect with the friends who invited me to El Salvador the first time: Joksan, from Hackerspace San Salvador. Joksan is one of the few people I admire, not only for his technical, scientific and mathematical knowledge of telecommunications and electronics, but because he's a walking book, very much his own person, who always uses exactly the right words in every situation. We talked about Nintendo, piracy, FPGAs, the black magic of technology, science and video games. It was so interesting that I forgot traffic in El Salvador is worse than in Guatemala, so I left Soyapango almost at 5 p.m. and a trip that's supposed to be 10 km took me almost two hours, HAHAHA.

When I got back, José was working, as always, and I started organizing things and beginning the change toward something I know I can do, even though I also know not doing it doesn't affect the result that much. So, for the coming days, remember this: I'm going to change my routine, set laziness aside and do the best I can, whenever I can.

No photos today, just delicious food in my head and an amazing dinner from Don Li: a ramen that, even though it was delivered, had a magnificent presentation.

Thanks for reading day four. See you on day five: we're no longer going to San Miguel, we're taking the coast so we can ride near El Salvador's beautiful beaches. Carretera del Litoral, here we come.
