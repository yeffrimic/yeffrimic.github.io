No excuses

Guatemala City → El Cerinal, Barberena · Guatemala · 47 km traveled

That same morning we almost didn't leave. One of the plans for this trip is to present José's book, "Adicto a la pobreza" (Addicted to Poverty), in Costa Rica. The books should already be in Guatemala, but they're stuck in customs: as of today, Monday, nobody has told us we can pick them up. We thought about staying in the city one more day and leaving on Monday with the books. We left it hanging for a while, until we both said: nah. This has to happen with or without books, and if we were already grabbing any excuse not to do it before even starting, that wasn't a good sign. So we left the excuses behind.

We left Guatemala City on Sunday, October 25, around noon. José took on the first kilometers by bike and I followed in the car. As I said in the first video: we're riding to Costa Rica by bike, but one of us doesn't know how to ride a bike. Guess who's in the car, haha. We still don't know what day we'll arrive.

At first we improvised a lot. We started streaming with OneStream, but José's camera kept going black on my end, haha, so we ended up riding as if on a call, but streaming. A few kilometers in we stopped to check everything again. The day before, I bought some microphones that have given us more headaches than help. But that's the interesting part: this is constant learning. We stopped again at a Shell gas station on the outskirts, on the highway to El Salvador, and went live on Facebook.

Then we got going again, little by little. From the car I watched José almost get hit several times: we were on the open highway and, even though the signs say 80 km/h maximum, cars easily passed at over 100. In Guatemala people don't respect those signs. The stretch was almost all downhill, with a couple of short climbs at the overpasses, but all good.

Around km 45 we decided to stop. About 25 hard-earned kilometers. To pack the bike we have to flip it upside down, with the seat and handlebars on the ground, and right then we heard something snap: the phone mount José was using for the live streams. It broke in two, so we couldn't stream anymore.

We were a few kilometers from El Cerinal, where my grandfather lives, so we went to spend the night with him: eating, laughing and learning a lot. We used the time to polish small details, like really understanding how the microphones work, and to charge everything that needed charging: phones, microphones, tripod.

View post →

Riding in the car behind José, I kept thinking that this is for real. We could turn back and say "at least we tried," but despite the pain ahead and not having a fully defined route, we're doing it: not perfectly, but little by little. José's determination is incredible. And I take away something else: help matters. Having someone to bounce ideas off, in this case me, helps you find better solutions together.
