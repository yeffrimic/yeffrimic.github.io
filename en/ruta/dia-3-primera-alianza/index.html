Chapter 3 · Day 3 · 27 Oct 2026

Walking more than we should almost cost us our first international partnership

Termaland, Ahuachapán → Zona Rosa, San Salvador · El Salvador · 100 km traveled

In the morning José was already working. I see him out of the corner of my eye, then he sees me and says: "You're awake?" And I'm like: "Yeees, but it's only six, it's early," HAHAHAHA. "Dude, it's 9," he tells me. And I'm like: "WHAAAAAT??" HAHAHAHA.

We sat down for breakfast and to talk. This trip isn't just for fun, so I started calling my contacts in El Salvador and Nicaragua, and now we might have partnerships in El Salvador, and we're looking for others in Nicaragua. But we needed a plan, because we weren't going to get there just like that.

We left the lovely Termaland hotel for the Malacatiupán waterfall in Ahuachapán, which are gorgeous: check out these photos and videos. We had to walk about 5 km each way under El Salvador's scorching sun, because we counted it as the day's workout. We crossed two bridges over clean rivers that didn't smell of sewage, unlike in Guatemala.

As we got close I told José: "It'd be amazing if they charged… well, are you carrying cash?" HAHAHA. The two dollars we had left from the night before. And he says: "I didn't bring them," HAHAHAHAHAHA. We got there and sure enough, it was 1.50 dollars per person. There were two of us, so three dollars: more than our entire budget at one point, which had also stayed back at the hotel, hahahahaha.

I asked if they took quetzales and the guard said nope, what were those anyway. I begged: "Look, we came all the way from Guatemala and we don't have dollars, take quetzales even if you charge us triple," haha. But he wouldn't. We were about to turn back when I asked: "Do you take cards?" "Sure," because they had a beautiful restaurant. So we said: "OK, charge us for the tickets and we'll order lunch while we're at it."

They let us in and we walked along a beautiful trail. We reached the top of the waterfall, kept going and… look at this beauty. It reminded me of Zelda, when you discover a new place. We went under through a small opening and boom: the glorious waterfalls. And you know the best part? They were hot water falls, HAHAHA.

You couldn't get under the waterfall because it was like a double cascade; you could only go in at the top, which was like a river. So we climbed up, I got in, and that's when I found out it was a hot river, haha. As you can imagine, there were big rocks baking in the sun that burned the soles of my feet. But there were some little pools and I thought: "I'll just hop in quickly," and boom, I almost fell, HAHAHAHAHAHA. José, far off in the shade, didn't even notice. Good thing, because how embarrassing, HAHAHA.

We had lunch. Just like in Guatemala, they have black corn tortillas here, and they're beautiful and really tasty. Chicken soup for me and a "completo" plate for José. Really good, honestly.

We walked back and now, finally, time to go. On the way, Kevin Chávez helped us get in touch with Miguel Chávez (no relation, haha) from the Universidad Evangélica de El Salvador, and we set a meeting for 3 p.m. Waze said it was two hours from where we were, and the plan was to leave the hotel at 1. WRONG: we left at 2:30. Very embarrassed, I texted Miguel: "Hey, we're going to be late, we didn't think it was so far."

In the end we went, and El Salvador's highway is so nice. We took a well-known route that, sure enough, was well maintained.

We got to the Universidad Evangélica, and I want to point out that it's sooo nice: huge, with lots of buildings and slopes so steep that, after the 10 or so km we'd walked a few hours earlier, we wanted nothing to do with them. We went down to the lowest part of campus and then had to climb back up on foot, backpacks and all, to meet Miguel. And there goes José, haha, who was doing better than me, because I arrived panting, HAHAHAHA.

Miguel welcomed us very kindly and we talked with him for almost an hour. In the end we agreed on what engineering students need: 1) mentors, 2) opportunities and 3) entrepreneurship. And that the CHIP Method has to lead to starting companies and solving problems. CHIP is the methodology we designed at Creabot to work on projects, in four steps: Context (truly understanding the problem), Hypothesis (proposing a solution you can test), Implementation (building it) and Presentation (showing and defending it). This conversation could lead to a partnership between UEES and Hazlo Ahora, and we want to find similar partnerships in Nicaragua.

Traffic in El Salvador is worse than in Guatemala. We were 2 km from the Airbnb and Waze said 1 hour 40 minutes, HAHAHAHA. So we stopped at a pupusería called Las Cumbres (so good): the best 20 dollars ever spent, by card, because we still had 2 dollars in cash, HAHAHAHA. After that the traffic dropped from almost two hours to 15 minutes. If I'd followed Waze… but since I went and got lost, it took us 30 minutes, HAHAHAHA.

In the end we made it and all good. The Airbnb is beautiful, and now, rest. Hectic, but all good. We'll see what we do tomorrow, buuut for today, end of story.
